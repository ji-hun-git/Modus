# Competitive and prior-art review — 28 October 2026

Research scope: official product documentation, company announcements, standards and author-published research. These are public claims, not independent product tests. No competitor account, private medical record, credential, held-out corpus or model API was accessed. Funding is evidence of a company's financing, not evidence that its product is accurate.

## Product decision

Modus should earn a position as a personal record workspace that makes messy historical documents inspectable, correctable and usable across time. “Upload records + timeline + AI chat” is already a competitive baseline. A defensible hypothesis is that explicit review, preserved source disagreements, historical availability replay and selective disclosure can help a person reconcile and reuse their history more reliably. That hypothesis needs task-based comparison; this review does not establish a first-ever invention or technical SOTA.

## Recent startup and platform signals

1. **Meta already has a health-record connection.** HealthEx announced its Muse connector on **8 September 2026**, for US users, with chosen care sites, consent and revocable connections. It describes visit summaries, doctor questions and follow-through. “Meta + your medical history” is therefore not a differentiator by itself. [HealthEx–Muse announcement](https://www.healthex.io/press/healthex-muse-partnership).
2. **Record access is becoming shared infrastructure.** On **27 October 2026**, HealthEx named ten emerging digital-health companies using its API/MCP platform. Its announcement claims access coverage and token savings; those figures are vendor claims, not measurements by Modus. The useful inference is that product teams are differentiating above the connection layer. Do not add MCP merely to match the announcement. [27 October announcement](https://www.healthex.io/press/healthex-emerging-digital-health-partnership).
3. **Function is a real adjacent unicorn, with a broader clinical business.** Its announcement reports **$298 million Series B at a $2.5 billion valuation**, and record uploads plus private chat alongside testing/imaging. The announcement dateline is **19 November 2025**; the web page shows publication **3 December 2025**. That is a historical financing valuation, not a verified current valuation. Its tests, clinicians and interventions are outside the bounded Modus contest entry. [Function announcement](https://www.functionhealth.com/article/function-announcement).
4. **Abridge competes on workflow integration and institutional distribution.** Its company newsroom recorded a **$300 million Series E** in June 2025; on **8 October 2026**, it announced enterprise-wide access across the University of Texas System. It serves clinicians, documentation and organizational workflows, so it is an adjacent execution benchmark rather than the same consumer product. [Company financing recap](https://www.abridge.com/blog/series-e-media-recap), [UT announcement](https://www.abridge.com/press-release/ut-health-enterprise-wide).

These signals do not justify trying to outbuild every funded company. A narrow, excellent owner task with credible evidence is more useful than copying their broad health claims into an ineligible contest entry.

## Technical novelty audit

| Proposed contribution | Prior art / actual claim boundary | Defensible next evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Two time axes | Valid/system time is established bitemporal database design. FHIR already distinguishes physiologically relevant observation time from when a version was made available to providers. [XTDB](https://docs.xtdb.com/about/time-in-xtdb.html), [FHIR R4 Observation](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/observation-definitions.html). | Specify exactly what Modus availability means, test late arrivals and later corrections, and show fewer historical evidence leaks than an event-date-only baseline. |
| Review and provenance | FHIR Provenance already represents activities and agents that create, revise, delete or sign a resource version. Source-linked review is an engineering choice, not a new provenance standard. [FHIR Provenance](https://hl7.org/fhir/R4/provenance.html). | Distinguish original report, extracted proposal, owner edit and committed fact; validate a portable provenance mapping and measure review burden. |
| Conflicts remain visible | Keeping competing assertions without selecting a winner is a useful policy, but this review does not establish it as novel. Same-date differences may reflect different samples or methods. | Measure false-conflict rate and missed disagreements; retain specimen/method/unit context and avoid silently merging records. |
| Evidence-constrained answers | Citation evaluation and grounded answers are established research and product capabilities. ALCE evaluates correctness and citation quality; a citation ID alone is insufficient. [ALCE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.14627). | Evaluate relevance, supported sentences, source coverage and abstention across all supported fact kinds, not only three featured metrics. |
| Document understanding | MedRepBench v2 (2 July 2026) covers 1,925 de-identified Chinese report images and field-level evaluation. OmniDocBench covers broad layouts, OCR, tables and ordering; its repository lists TeleOCR results added 11 September 2026. Neither makes Modus SOTA by association. [MedRepBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.16674), [OmniDocBench](https://github.com/opendatalab/OmniDocBench). | Compare parser/OCR, Spark and Spark+verification on reviewed Korean/English development cases with exact value/unit/date errors, coverage, review time, latency and cost. Keep existing held-outs unopened. |

Important semantic correction: a review commit establishes **available in Modus's reviewed history**, not when the person or their clinician first knew the information. It also does not make an edited number medically true. Keep report issuance, receipt/import and review time separate wherever those dates are available; preserve uncertainty rather than inventing an earlier knowledge time.

## Privacy as product behavior

Guava publicly claims encryption and controls, while its policy also identifies processing providers and possible authorized staff access. Its provider FAQ says its AI-provider agreements prohibit storage/training. These are different assurances from end-to-end encryption and should not be conflated. [Guava policy](https://guavahealth.com/privacy), [security page](https://guavahealth.com/privacy-and-security), [provider FAQ](https://guavahealth.com/provider-dashboard-faq).

Proposed Modus acceptance criteria: an owner can see the exact text/images about to leave the device; keep originals and identifiers local where possible; grant a narrow per-task disclosure; cancel or use the local route; inspect an access receipt; and delete originals, derived facts, caches and exports according to a defined policy. Redacting names alone is not a demonstrated anonymity guarantee. Retained dates and rare conditions can identify a person, and cloud vision receives pixels unless they are actually removed before transmission. These are proposed controls, not claims about today's prototype.

## A measurable way to compete

Use the same consented or authored file pack and ask people to: find a childhood vaccination, locate an exact previous result and source page, identify a disagreement without choosing a winner, explain what became newly available, and assemble a relevant visit packet. Measure successful completion, time, clicks, corrections and disclosure volume. Separate document accuracy from interface task success.

The strongest current pitch is: **Modus helps you turn scattered reports into a history you can inspect, correct and carry forward—while keeping the original evidence and what changed.** The proposed research contribution is an evaluated combination of record-review, temporal evidence controls and privacy-aware retrieval. “World first,” “best agent,” “SOTA accuracy,” clinical validation, guaranteed anonymity and superior usability remain unproven.
